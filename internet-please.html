About this game

Every home in town is asking the same thing.

Internet, please.

Internet, Please is a minimalist game about bringing the internet to a small town. You are its new internet provider, and homes keep popping up, each one waiting to get online. Draw cables from the Internet to their doors, link them through switches and routers, and watch the town come alive. Every video, song, video call and game is a little packet gliding through glass cables all the way home.

Then evening comes, everyone streams at once, and your tidy network starts to sweat.

Designed by a network engineer. Made for everyone.

Features

Calm map. Loud lines.

Build the network

  • Draw cables along the streets, then upgrade copper to fiber and fast fiber for more lanes.
  • Place switches and routers to gather homes and carry heavy traffic. New devices arrive as the town grows.
  • Cross the water with a wireless link, or rent a line from another provider for far-off homes.

Real network rules, made simple

  • Packets take the quickest path, and equal paths share the load. Build a quicker one and they switch to it.
  • Every cable has lanes. When they fill, packets queue. When the queue fills, packets drop.
  • Push a device too hard and it glows orange, then red, and starts losing packets. Upgrade it, and it carries more.
  • Every jam is right there on the map. No spreadsheets, no command line, no networking knowledge needed.

Keep every home happy

  • Faces tell you everything. Slow or lost packets make a home worried, then grumpy. If too many give up and leave, the town is lost.
  • Read the signs. A home puts on headphones, picks up a controller or turns on the TV, and a swarm of packets follows a moment later.
  • Every home has its own taste: movies, live streams, music, video calls, games, shopping and more.

Every coin counts

  • Happy homes pay for what they use. Grumpy homes stop paying.
  • Spend your coins on cables, devices and upgrades, right where the traffic needs them.
  • A rented line costs the most, and the other provider keeps a share of what its homes pay.

Real cities

  • Start in a quiet Old Town, then win your way to real cities like Berlin, Venice, New York and Hong Kong.
  • Rivers, seas and mountains decide where your cables can go.
  • Every win brings records and badges.

What is Internet, Please?

A minimalist game about bringing the internet to a growing town. You are the new internet provider: you draw cables from the Internet to homes, offices and game cafés, place switches and routers, and keep everyone happy when the whole town streams at once.

Do I need to know anything about networks?

No. The game uses real network rules, made simple: packets take the quickest path, cables have lanes, and busy devices glow orange and then red. Every jam is right there on the map. No spreadsheets, no command line, no networking knowledge needed.

Which platforms is it coming to?

Steam, for Windows, macOS and Linux. Wishlist it on Steam to hear the moment it is out.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. It is single player and plays fully offline, ready for a train ride or a long flight.

Which languages does it speak?

English, German, French, Spanish, Japanese and Korean.

Is it like Mini Metro or Mini Motorways?

If you enjoy calm, minimalist puzzle games where you draw lines across a growing map, you will feel at home. Instead of trains or roads, you lay glass cables full of colourful packets, and the pressure comes from the evening streaming rush.

Who makes it?

Firewall Games, a small independent studio. Internet, Please was designed by a network engineer and made for everyone.
