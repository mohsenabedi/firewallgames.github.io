The premise

Every home in town is asking the same thing: Internet, please.

You are the town's new internet provider. Homes keep popping up, each one waiting to get online. Faces tell you everything: slow or lost packets make a home worried, then grumpy. If too many give up and leave, the town is lost.

How it plays

Real network rules, made simple.

Every video, song, video call and game is a little packet gliding through glass cables all the way home. No spreadsheets, no command line, no networking knowledge needed.

Build the network.

  • Draw cables along the streets, then upgrade copper to fiber and fast fiber for more lanes.
  • Place switches and routers to gather homes and carry heavy traffic. New devices arrive as the town grows.
  • Cross the water with a wireless link, or rent a line from another provider for far-off homes.

Read the jam on the map.

  • Packets take the quickest path, and equal paths share the load. Build a quicker one and they switch to it.
  • Every cable has lanes. When they fill, packets queue. When the queue fills, packets drop.
  • Push a device too hard and it glows orange, then red, and starts losing packets. Upgrade it, and it carries more.

Keep every home happy.

  • Read the signs. A home puts on headphones, picks up a controller or turns on the TV, and a swarm of packets follows a moment later.
  • Every home has its own taste: movies, live streams, music, video calls, games, shopping and more. Offices live on video calls, game cafés on games.

Every coin counts.

  • Happy homes pay for what they use. Grumpy homes stop paying.
  • Spend your coins on cables, devices and upgrades, right where the traffic needs them.
  • A rented line costs the most, and the other provider keeps a share of what its homes pay.

Day and dusk

Then evening comes.

Morning. Your tidy network hums along, one cable at a time.

Afternoon. Offices fill up with video calls. Game cafés start to buzz.

Evening. Everyone streams at once, and your tidy network starts to sweat.

Real cities

Start in a quiet Old Town. Win your way to the world.

Rivers, seas and mountains decide where your cables can go. Every win brings records and badges.

Amsterdam

Three rings of canals circle the Dam. Cables cross only at the bridges; Noord waits across the IJ.

2

Berlin

The Spree cuts the city in two. Cables stop at the water; wireless crosses it.

3

Venice

One causeway to the mainland, two bridges over the Grand Canal. Every other island is a hop over the lagoon.

4

New York

Manhattan is long and thin, and every other borough is across a river.

5

Hong Kong

Central is a strip between the Peak and the harbour. Kowloon is one hop away.

What flows through the cables

Every video, song, call and game is a little packet.

The glyph, not the colour, tells them apart, so the town reads at a glance in any light.
